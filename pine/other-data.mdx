## Another timeframe or symbol

`request.security()` reads another timeframe of the chart's symbol, or another symbol that Darvas lists, such as a crypto pair.

```pine
//@version=6
indicator("Daily close on intraday", overlay=true)
dailyClose = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", close)
plot(dailyClose, "Daily close", color=color.orange)
```

- `syminfo.tickerid` and the chart's own pair read the chart's market.
- Another symbol is matched against the markets Darvas lists. A ticker matches without separators, so `ETHUSDT` finds `ETH-USDT`. The `.P` suffix asks for the perpetual. Without it, spot comes first.
- An exchange prefix, such as `OKX:` in `OKX:ETHUSDT`, picks the exchange that Darvas reads first, when your account can chart that exchange. The chart's exchange comes next. A prefix of an exchange that Darvas does not list, such as `COINBASE:`, stops the script with "`COINBASE` in `COINBASE:BTCUSD` is not an exchange on Darvas".
- A symbol Darvas does not list, such as `DXY` or `SPY`, stops the script with "`DXY` is not available on Darvas".
- Timeframes can be seconds, minutes, `D`, `W` and `M`, including non-standard ones such as `3D`. An unsupported timeframe stops the script with an error that names it.
- A series covers the chart back to its first bar. A lower timeframe reads up to 5,000 bars, so on long charts the oldest bars can read `na`. A higher timeframe reads at least 500 of its own bars.
- One run makes at most 12 data reads. Each distinct `request.*()` series counts as one read, and so does `indicator(timeframe=...)`.

## Run the whole script on another timeframe

`indicator(timeframe="D")` runs the script on that timeframe of the chart's symbol and places the values on the chart's bars, as on TradingView.

- Write the timeframe as a string literal.
- `timeframe_gaps` works as on TradingView. Gaps are on by default.
- Labels, lines, boxes, tables, polylines and linefills cannot be combined with `timeframe=`. A script that draws them fails with an error.

## US company data

These calls read US companies' SEC filings. Each value appears on the day its filing was public. A later restatement changes the value from its own filing day, and earlier bars keep what was filed.

| Call | What it returns |
| --- | --- |
| `request.financial(symbol, id, period)` | A reported value for `period` `FQ`, `FY` or `TTM`. |
| `request.earnings(symbol, earnings.actual)` | Each quarter's diluted EPS, from its first filing. |
| `request.dividends(symbol, dividends.gross)` | The cash dividend per share as paid, from its ex-date. Later splits do not adjust it. |
| `request.splits(symbol, splits.numerator)` | A split's ratio from its first session. `splits.denominator` gives the other side. |

The `request.financial` ids are `TOTAL_REVENUE`, `GROSS_PROFIT`, `OPER_INCOME`, `NET_INCOME`, `EBITDA`, `EARNINGS_PER_SHARE_BASIC`, `EARNINGS_PER_SHARE_DILUTED`, `DPS_COMMON_STOCK_PRIM_ISSUE`, `TOTAL_ASSETS`, `TOTAL_DEBT`, `TOTAL_EQUITY`, `CASH_N_EQUIVALENTS`, `TOTAL_SHARES_OUTSTANDING`, `DILUTED_SHARES_OUTSTANDING`, `CASH_F_OPERATING_ACTIVITIES`, `CAPITAL_EXPENDITURES`, `FREE_CASH_FLOW`, `BOOK_VALUE_PER_SHARE`, `GROSS_MARGIN` and `RETURN_ON_EQUITY`. `GROSS_MARGIN` and `RETURN_ON_EQUITY` are percentages.

The symbol is an SEC ticker, with or without a US exchange prefix, such as `NASDAQ:AAPL` or `NYSE:BRK.B`. On an OKX stock swap or a Bybit xStocks chart, `syminfo.tickerid` names the stock that the market tracks. Any other symbol stops the script with "is not a US company on Darvas".

```pine
//@version=6
indicator("P/E", overlay=false)
eps = request.financial(syminfo.tickerid, "EARNINGS_PER_SHARE_DILUTED", "TTM", ignore_invalid_symbol=true)
plot(close / eps, "P/E")
```

<Note>
  An xStocks close is the price of one token, and one token may not equal one share. A ratio such as P/E then needs the token's share ratio.
</Note>

## US economic data

`request.economic("US", field)` reads these fields, each from the day it was published:

| Field | Series |
| --- | --- |
| `GDPQQ` | Real GDP growth, each quarter |
| `IRYY` | Inflation rate: CPI change over 12 months |
| `CIR` | Core inflation rate: core CPI change over 12 months |
| `CPI` | Consumer price index, not seasonally adjusted |
| `CCPI` | Core consumer price index, not seasonally adjusted |
| `PCEPI` | PCE price index |
| `CPCEPI` | Core PCE price index |
| `UR` | Unemployment rate |
| `NFP` | Non-farm payrolls: the change from the month before |
| `LFPR` | Labor force participation rate |
| `EMR` | Employment rate: the employment to population ratio |
| `AWH` | Average weekly hours |
| `INTR` | Interest rate: the midpoint of the federal funds target range. TradingView shows the upper bound. |

## Values that read na

- `earnings.estimate`, `earnings.standardized`, `dividends.net`, the period `FH`, the `currency` argument, other countries and other economic fields read `na`.
- With `ignore_invalid_symbol=true`, `request.financial`, `request.earnings`, `request.dividends` and `request.splits` read `na` for a symbol Darvas cannot serve, such as a crypto pair, instead of stopping the script.
- `request.economic` reads `na` when Darvas cannot serve the field.
- With `barmerge.gaps_on`, a value shows only on the bar where it changed. Without it, the value holds on every later bar.
